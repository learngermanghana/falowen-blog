## Partizip I or Partizip II? Start with the meaning

German learners often see phrases such as **eine prägende Kultur**, **aufwachsende Kinder**, **geprägte Menschen** and **eine wachsende Wirtschaft** and wonder why some forms contain a **d** while others do not.

The basic distinction is useful: **Partizip I normally presents an action or process as ongoing or active, while Partizip II often presents a completed action, result or passive meaning.** When either form comes before a noun, it behaves like an adjective and receives an adjective ending.

## Partizip I: infinitive + d

Form Partizip I by adding **d** to the infinitive:

- wachsen → **wachsend**
- lernen → **lernend**
- arbeiten → **arbeitend**
- tanzen → **tanzend**
- prägen → **prägend**

The meaning is often similar to English **-ing**:

- **die tanzende Frau** = the dancing woman
- **ein wachsendes Unternehmen** = a growing company
- **eine prägende Erfahrung** = a formative or influential experience

The **d** belongs to Partizip I itself. Before a noun, an adjective ending comes after it: **lernend → ein lernender Schüler → eine lernende Schülerin**.

## Partizip II: completed action or result

Partizip II is the form also used in the Perfekt:

- machen → **gemacht**
- prägen → **geprägt**
- schreiben → **geschrieben**
- öffnen → **geöffnet**
- wachsen → **gewachsen**

As an adjective, it often expresses a result or something that has happened to the noun:

- **die geöffnete Tür** = the opened/open door
- **der geschriebene Bericht** = the written report
- **die kulturell geprägte Gesellschaft** = the society shaped by culture
- **die in Ghana aufgewachsenen Menschen** = the people who grew up in Ghana

## Compare the meaning

**eine prägende Kultur** means a culture that shapes or influences people. **eine kulturell geprägte Gesellschaft** means a society that has been shaped by culture.

Likewise, **eine wachsende Wirtschaft** is an economy that is currently growing, while **eine stark gewachsene Wirtschaft** is an economy that has grown significantly.

Another useful pair is **die sich verändernde Arbeitswelt** (the changing world of work) and **die veränderten Arbeitsbedingungen** (the changed working conditions).

## Adjective endings still apply

Participles before nouns follow the same adjective-declension rules as ordinary adjectives. Compare **der interessante Kurs**, **der laufende Kurs** and **der abgeschlossene Kurs**.

Examples:

- Ich spreche mit **den teilnehmenden Studierenden**. — I am speaking with the participating students.
- Die Regierung unterstützt **die betroffenen Familien**. — The government supports the affected families.
- Wir diskutieren über **eine zunehmend digitalisierte Arbeitswelt**. — We are discussing an increasingly digitalised world of work.

## Useful forms for B2 and C1

Learn these as complete noun phrases:

- **steigende Preise** — rising prices
- **sinkende Kosten** — falling costs
- **wachsende Nachfrage** — growing demand
- **zunehmende Belastung** — increasing pressure
- **betroffene Beschäftigte** — affected employees
- **qualifizierte Fachkräfte** — qualified skilled workers
- **digitalisierte Prozesse** — digitalised processes
- **anerkannte Qualifikationen** — recognised qualifications
- **verbesserte Arbeitsbedingungen** — improved working conditions

At advanced levels, participles can also make writing more compact. Instead of **Menschen, die in einem mehrsprachigen Umfeld aufwachsen**, you can write **in einem mehrsprachigen Umfeld aufwachsende Menschen**. Use this style when it improves precision; clarity is more important than complexity.

## Common mistakes

A frequent mistake is using Partizip II for an ongoing action. If you mean “the dancing woman,” write **die tanzende Frau**, not **die getanzte Frau**.

Another mistake is forgetting the adjective ending: **eine wachsend Wirtschaft** is incorrect; **eine wachsende Wirtschaft** is correct.

Finally, do not translate every English **-ing** word mechanically. German may prefer a normal adjective, noun or relative clause depending on context.

## A quick decision method

Ask yourself:

1. Is the noun actively doing something, or is a process happening? Partizip I is often suitable.
2. Has something happened to the noun, or am I describing a result? Partizip II is often suitable.
3. Is the participle directly before a noun? Add the correct adjective ending.

For example, **Die Kultur prägt die Menschen** leads naturally to **eine prägende Kultur**. By contrast, **Die Kultur hat die Menschen geprägt** can lead to **kulturell geprägte Menschen**.

## Mini practice

Choose the correct form:

1. eine ______ Wirtschaft (wachsen / gewachsen)
2. die von der Krise ______ Unternehmen (betreffen / betroffen)
3. ein ______ Kind (lachen / gelacht)
4. die in Deutschland ______ Fachkraft (ausbilden / ausgebildet)
5. eine die Gesellschaft ______ Entwicklung (verändern / verändert)

Possible answers: **wachsende**, **betroffenen**, **lachendes**, **ausgebildete**, **verändernde**.

## Practical learner tip

Do not memorise participles only as isolated grammar rules. Learn useful combinations such as **steigende Preise**, **wachsende Nachfrage**, **betroffene Menschen**, **ausgebildete Fachkräfte** and **prägende Erfahrungen**. This trains meaning, vocabulary and adjective endings at the same time.
