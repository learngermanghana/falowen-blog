# Falowen Now Offers Swahili: A New Language to Learn Online

**Falowen is expanding its language-learning experience. Alongside German, learners can now explore Swahili (Kiswahili) on Falowen.**

This is an exciting addition for students who want to connect with people and cultures across East Africa. Our goal is to make beginner Swahili approachable through clear lessons, practical conversations and opportunities to practise—not just lists of words to memorise.

We are introducing the course progressively, beginning with **Swahili A1 content**. More lessons and learning resources will follow as the programme grows.

## Why learn Swahili?

Swahili, also known as **Kiswahili**, is widely spoken in East Africa, including Tanzania and Kenya, and is used in communities across the region.

Whether you are curious about a new language, planning to visit East Africa, interested in African cultures or simply want to communicate with more people, Swahili offers a rewarding place to start.

You do not need to know Swahili before beginning the introductory lessons.

## What can you learn on Falowen?

Falowen is bringing its structured learning approach to Kiswahili. The developing beginner course introduces learning through a familiar Course Book interface with sections such as:

- **Vocabulary and useful expressions** for everyday communication
- **Grammar explanations** to help you understand sentence patterns
- **Listening activities** to hear spoken Kiswahili in context
- **Interactive workbook practice** to check what you understand
- **Speaking and conversation tasks** to help you use what you have learned

The programme is being built lesson by lesson, so the subjects and activities available can vary as new content is released. It is not yet a complete A1–C2 Swahili catalogue.

## Learn words you can use in real conversations

Here are a few useful beginner expressions:

| Kiswahili | English |
|---|---|
| Habari? | How are things? / What's the news? |
| Nzuri. | Fine / Good. |
| Asante. | Thank you. |
| Tafadhali. | Please. |
| Samahani. | Excuse me / Sorry. |
| Jina langu ni … | My name is … |
| Naomba maji. | I would like some water, please. |

A good way to practise is to learn a short exchange instead of memorising each expression separately.

**A:** Habari yako?  
**B:** Nzuri, asante. Na wewe?  
**A:** Nzuri pia.

*How are you? — Fine, thank you. And you? — Fine too.*

Say the dialogue aloud, swap roles and try again without looking at the translation.

## Everyday situations: learning at a restaurant

Practical situations help new words become useful. For example, a simple restaurant conversation can introduce words such as **maji** (water), **chakula** (food) and **naomba** (a polite way to ask for something).

You might say:

> **Naomba maji, tafadhali.**  
> Water, please.

Or:

> **Ninapenda wali.**  
> I like rice.

These are short sentences you can adapt as your vocabulary grows. Listening to natural conversations and practising your own replies are important parts of learning to communicate.

## Can existing German students also study Swahili?

Falowen is designed to support multiple language programmes. Existing learners can keep their German learning separate from any additional Swahili enrolment or access granted to their account.

Your available lessons depend on your assigned programme and current access. If Swahili does not appear in your account, check with the Falowen team rather than assuming your German enrolment automatically includes it.

## A growing course, not a finished catalogue

We are building Kiswahili carefully using the established Falowen learning structure while creating materials suited to Swahili pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar and conversation.

The focus right now is **beginner-level learning**. We will expand the topics and activities gradually instead of suggesting that every lesson and advanced level is already available.

## How to get started

1. Visit [Falowen](https://www.falowen.app/).
2. Open the [Swahili course page](https://www.falowen.app/swahili) to explore the currently available introduction.
3. If you are registering, choose Swahili where the language selector is offered.
4. Open the Course Book available to your account and begin with the introductory lessons.

If you already have a Falowen account, sign in first. For questions about Swahili access or live teaching arrangements, contact the team through Falowen before making any enrolment or payment decisions.
