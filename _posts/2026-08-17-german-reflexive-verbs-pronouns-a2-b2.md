## Why German reflexive verbs matter

If you are learning German at A2, B1 or B2, you will regularly meet sentences such as **Ich interessiere mich für Musik**, **Wir treffen uns um acht Uhr** and **Ich wasche mir die Hände**. The small words **mich, uns** and **mir** are reflexive pronouns, and using them correctly makes your German much more natural.

A reflexive verb describes an action that refers back to the subject. In English, this idea sometimes appears with *myself, yourself* or *ourselves*, but German uses reflexive pronouns more often than English does.

For example:

- **Ich freue mich.** = I am happy / I am pleased.
- **Du beeilst dich.** = You are hurrying.
- **Wir treffen uns morgen.** = We are meeting tomorrow.

The key is not to translate every reflexive pronoun literally. Instead, learn the German verb together with its reflexive pronoun and, when necessary, its preposition.

## 1. The accusative reflexive pronouns

Most reflexive verbs use the accusative.

| Subject | Reflexive pronoun | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ich | mich | Ich erinnere **mich**. |
| du | dich | Du beeilst **dich**. |
| er / sie / es | sich | Er konzentriert **sich**. |
| wir | uns | Wir treffen **uns**. |
| ihr | euch | Ihr setzt **euch**. |
| sie / Sie | sich | Sie freuen **sich**. |

Notice that only **ich** and **du** have special-looking accusative forms here. The third-person forms use **sich**.

### Useful examples

- **Ich interessiere mich für Deutsch.** = I am interested in German.
- **Sie freut sich auf die Reise.** = She is looking forward to the trip.
- **Wir unterhalten uns über den Film.** = We are talking about the film.
- **Beeilst du dich morgens?** = Do you hurry in the morning?

## 2. When do you use mir and dir instead of mich and dich?

This is one of the most useful distinctions for intermediate learners. A reflexive pronoun can be dative when the sentence already has another accusative object.

Compare:

- **Ich wasche mich.** = I wash myself.
- **Ich wasche mir die Hände.** = I wash my hands.

In the second sentence, **die Hände** is the accusative object. The reflexive pronoun therefore appears as **mir**.

The dative reflexive forms are:

| Subject | Dative reflexive pronoun |
| --- | --- |
| ich | mir |
| du | dir |
| er / sie / es | sich |
| wir | uns |
| ihr | euch |
| sie / Sie | sich |

More examples:

- **Du putzt dir die Zähne.** = You brush your teeth.
- **Er zieht sich eine Jacke an.** = He puts on a jacket.
- **Ich kaufe mir ein neues Wörterbuch.** = I buy myself a new dictionary.

A practical question to ask yourself is: **Is there already an accusative object in the sentence?** If yes, check whether the reflexive pronoun should be dative.

## 3. Some verbs are normally learned as reflexive verbs

Certain German verbs are best memorised with **sich** because the reflexive form is part of the expression.

Important examples include:

- **sich beeilen** — to hurry
- **sich erinnern an + Akk.** — to remember
- **sich interessieren für + Akk.** — to be interested in
- **sich konzentrieren auf + Akk.** — to concentrate on
- **sich entscheiden für / gegen + Akk.** — to decide for / against
- **sich beschweren über + Akk.** — to complain about
- **sich vorbereiten auf + Akk.** — to prepare for
- **sich kümmern um + Akk.** — to take care of

Do not learn only **interessieren**. Learn the complete chunk **sich für etwas interessieren**.

For example:

**Ich interessiere mich für deutsche Geschichte.**  
*I am interested in German history.*

That learning method helps you remember the pronoun, the preposition and the case at the same time.

## 4. Reflexive verbs with prepositions

At B1 and B2, many mistakes happen because learners remember the reflexive pronoun but forget the fixed preposition.

Consider these pairs:

- **sich freuen auf + Akk.** = to look forward to something in the future
- **sich freuen über + Akk.** = to be happy about something

Examples:

**Ich freue mich auf das Wochenende.**  
I am looking forward to the weekend.

**Ich freue mich über dein Geschenk.**  
I am happy about your gift.

Another useful pair is:

**Wir bereiten uns auf die Prüfung vor.**  
We are preparing for the exam.

Here, **vorbereiten** is also separable: the prefix **vor** moves to the end in a main clause.

## 5. Word order with reflexive pronouns

In a normal main clause, the reflexive pronoun usually stays close to the conjugated verb.

- **Ich interessiere mich für Sprachen.**
- **Heute interessiere ich mich besonders für Aussprache.**
- **Warum beschwerst du dich über den Kurs?**

With a subordinate clause, the conjugated verb moves to the end, but the reflexive pronoun stays with the subject area of the clause:

**Ich lerne jeden Tag, weil ich mich auf die Prüfung vorbereite.**  
I study every day because I am preparing for the exam.

At B1 and B2, this combination of reflexive verbs and subordinate-clause word order is especially useful in writing and speaking tasks.

## 6. Common mistakes German learners make

### Mistake 1: forgetting the reflexive pronoun

Incorrect: **Ich interessiere für Musik.**  
Correct: **Ich interessiere mich für Musik.**

### Mistake 2: translating directly from English

English says *I remember the meeting*, but German normally uses:

**Ich erinnere mich an das Treffen.**

Not every German structure matches English word for word.

### Mistake 3: using mich when dative is needed

Incorrect: **Ich putze mich die Zähne.**  
Correct: **Ich putze mir die Zähne.**

Because **die Zähne** is already the accusative object, **mir** is used.

### Mistake 4: learning the verb without its preposition

Instead of memorising **vorbereiten = prepare**, learn:

**sich auf etwas vorbereiten = to prepare for something**

This is much more useful when you need to build your own sentence.

## 7. A2, B1 and B2: how your use should develop

At **A2**, focus on common everyday reflexive verbs:

- sich waschen
- sich anziehen
- sich treffen
- sich freuen
- sich beeilen

At **B1**, add fixed prepositions and longer sentences:

**Ich bereite mich auf meine Deutschprüfung vor, weil ich nächstes Jahr in Deutschland studieren möchte.**

At **B2**, use reflexive verbs to express opinions, decisions, responsibilities and abstract ideas:

**Viele Lernende entscheiden sich für einen Intensivkurs, damit sie sich gezielt auf ihre Sprachprüfung vorbereiten können.**

This type of sentence gives you more precise vocabulary and more varied grammar in exam writing and speaking.

## 8. Mini practice

Choose the correct reflexive pronoun.

1. Ich interessiere ___ für deutsche Musik.
2. Du wäschst ___ die Hände.
3. Wir freuen ___ auf die Ferien.
4. Er bereitet ___ auf die Prüfung vor.
5. Ihr müsst ___ beeilen.

Answers:

1. **mich**
2. **dir**
3. **uns**
4. **sich**
5. **euch**

Now make one personal sentence with each of these expressions: **sich interessieren für**, **sich freuen auf**, **sich vorbereiten auf** and **sich entscheiden für**.

## Practical learner tips

Learn reflexive verbs as complete phrases rather than isolated vocabulary. Say **sich für etwas interessieren**, not simply **interessieren**. When a preposition belongs to the verb, memorise the case as well. Then create a personal example immediately; a sentence about your own studies, work or plans is easier to remember than a random textbook sentence.

Also practise the same verb in different persons: **ich freue mich, du freust dich, wir freuen uns**. This prevents you from treating **sich** as the only possible reflexive pronoun.

Finally, compare accusative and dative pairs aloud: **Ich wasche mich** versus **Ich wasche mir die Hände**. Hearing the contrast repeatedly makes the pattern easier to use automatically.
