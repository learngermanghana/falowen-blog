**Learning German does not stop when a lesson ends.** Sometimes you are studying at home, meet an unfamiliar word or finish a letter, and need to know what to improve before your next class.

The goal is not to let AI do your homework. It is to help you understand mistakes, ask better questions and improve your own German.

## 1. Study Buddy: an AI German tutor when you need an explanation

Imagine you are reading a German text and encounter the verb **abbauen**. Depending on the context, it can mean *to dismantle*, *to reduce* or *to take down*. If you are still learning, that difference matters.

A Falowen learner recently asked Study Buddy about this exact word. The assistant explained its meanings and gave a German example. The real mobile photo below shows that conversation.

Study Buddy is designed for quick help with:

- **German vocabulary:** Learn what words mean in context, rather than memorising one English translation.
- **Grammar questions:** Ask why a verb moves to the end of a sentence or why a particular article is used.
- **German sentence examples:** See how a rule works in a practical expression.
- **Follow-up questions:** Continue the explanation when something is still unclear.

It also gives students shortcuts to course materials, results and other learning areas within Falowen.

### A simple grammar example

Consider the German sentence:

*Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich krank bin.*

Why is **bin** at the end? Because **weil** introduces a subordinate clause in which the conjugated verb normally comes last. Instead of only learning the answer, you can use Study Buddy to understand the rule and practise another example.

## 2. Mark My Letter: feedback that helps you improve your German writing

Writing a German email or letter is a key challenge for many A1, A2 and B1 learners. You may know the vocabulary but still struggle with word order, the appropriate greeting or whether you answered every point in a writing task.

**Mark My Letter** is Falowen's AI-assisted German writing feedback tool. Its purpose is to help students look again at their own writing and identify what needs improvement.

For instance, a learner might write:

*Ich schreibe dir, weil ich bin krank.*

A corrected version is:

*Ich schreibe dir, weil ich krank bin.*

The important lesson is the **word-order rule**, not just the correction. When reviewing a German letter, students should also check whether they communicated every required detail and used a suitable tone.

### How to get more value from German writing feedback

Write the letter yourself first. Review the suggestions, identify which errors you understand, then rewrite the sentence or message without copying the correction. That practice helps turn feedback into independent writing skills.

AI marking is a learning aid, not an official Goethe-Institut assessment. Teacher feedback and the specific requirements of your course still matter.

## 4. A practical weekly routine

Here is a simple approach for getting the most out of these tools:

1. **Study the assigned lesson** in Falowen.
2. **Ask Study Buddy** about a rule or word you do not understand.
3. **Write your own German sentences, email or letter** using what you learned.
4. **Review the writing** with Mark My Letter where available.
5. **Try again independently**, then ask your teacher about anything still unclear.

Better models can help explain more clearly, but steady practice is still what helps you make progress.
