## Why German word order feels difficult

You may know the right German vocabulary and still produce a sentence that sounds unusual. Often, the problem is not the words themselves but **where you put them**.

A useful pattern for German learners is **TeKaMoLo**. It helps you organize information about **time, cause, manner and place**. It is especially useful from A2 onward and can make B1 and B2 speaking and writing much clearer.

## What does TeKaMoLo mean?

**TeKaMoLo** stands for:

- **Te – temporal:** When?
- **Ka – kausal:** Why?
- **Mo – modal:** How?
- **Lo – lokal:** Where?

A common neutral order is therefore:

**Time → Cause → Manner → Place**

Example:

**Ich fahre morgen wegen eines Termins mit dem Bus nach Accra.**  
*I am travelling to Accra tomorrow by bus because of an appointment.*

Here we have:

- **morgen** = temporal
- **wegen eines Termins** = kausal
- **mit dem Bus** = modal
- **nach Accra** = lokal

TeKaMoLo is a useful default pattern, not a rule that makes every other order impossible. German speakers can move information for emphasis.

## The verb still controls the sentence

TeKaMoLo does not replace the basic German word-order rules.

In a normal main clause, the conjugated verb is usually in **position 2**:

**Ich lerne heute zu Hause Deutsch.**  
*I am studying German at home today.*

You can move the time expression to the beginning:

**Heute lerne ich zu Hause Deutsch.**  
*Today I am studying German at home.*

Notice what happened. **Heute** takes position 1, so **lerne** remains in position 2 and the subject **ich** comes after the verb.

A common learner mistake is:

❌ **Heute ich lerne Deutsch.**  
✅ **Heute lerne ich Deutsch.**

## TeKaMoLo with several details

Consider this sentence:

**Wir treffen uns am Samstag wegen der Prüfung gemeinsam in der Bibliothek.**  
*We are meeting on Saturday because of the exam, together, in the library.*

The information follows the neutral pattern:

**am Samstag** (time) → **wegen der Prüfung** (cause) → **gemeinsam** (manner) → **in der Bibliothek** (place).

You do not need all four elements in every sentence. If you only have time and place, the common pattern is simply time before place:

**Ich arbeite heute im Büro.**  
*I am working in the office today.*

**Wir lernen jeden Abend zu Hause.**  
*We study at home every evening.*

## What happens in a subordinate clause?

With conjunctions such as **weil, obwohl, dass** and **wenn**, the conjugated verb normally moves to the end.

Main clause:

**Ich bleibe heute wegen des Wetters zu Hause.**  
*I am staying at home today because of the weather.*

With **weil**:

**Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil das Wetter heute sehr schlecht ist.**  
*I am staying at home because the weather is very bad today.*

This distinction is important for B1, B2 and C1 learners: first identify the clause type, then organize the other information.

## Three common word-order mistakes

### 1. Forgetting verb position after a time expression

❌ **Morgen ich gehe zur Arbeit.**  
✅ **Morgen gehe ich zur Arbeit.**

### 2. Copying English word order directly

German and English often organize sentence information differently. Translate the **meaning**, then rebuild the sentence according to German structure instead of translating word for word.

### 3. Treating TeKaMoLo as an absolute law

TeKaMoLo describes a useful neutral order. Context and emphasis can change the order. Learn the default first; as your German improves, pay attention to how native speakers rearrange elements to highlight particular information.

## A practical way to practise

Take one simple sentence and expand it step by step:

**Ich lerne Deutsch.**  
*I learn German.*

Add time:

**Ich lerne jeden Abend Deutsch.**

Add manner:

**Ich lerne jeden Abend konzentriert Deutsch.**

Add place:

**Ich lerne jeden Abend konzentriert zu Hause Deutsch.**

Then practise beginning with the time expression:

**Jeden Abend lerne ich konzentriert zu Hause Deutsch.**

This exercise trains both TeKaMoLo and the verb-second rule.

## Quick practice

Put these parts into a natural German sentence:

**ich / morgen / mit meiner Freundin / in der Bibliothek / lerne**

A good answer is:

**Ich lerne morgen mit meiner Freundin in der Bibliothek.**

Now move **morgen** to position 1:

**Morgen lerne ich mit meiner Freundin in der Bibliothek.**

The most important thing to notice is that the verb remains in position 2.
