**Karibu, Dr. Ruth!** Falowen is pleased to introduce **Dr. Ruth Ndekiro Shao**, our new **Kiswahili (Swahili) language instructor and researcher**.

For learners in **Ghana, Nigeria and across Africa** who want to learn Swahili online, this is a chance to discover the language with guidance informed by years of teaching and research.

Swahili is not only a language to study. It is a way to connect with people, stories and communities across East and Central Africa.

## Who is Dr. Ruth Ndekiro Shao?

Dr. Ruth is an experienced Kiswahili educator with a strong academic background and a practical approach to language teaching.

Her background includes:

- **PhD in Kiswahili** from the **University of Dar es Salaam**, Tanzania
- **More than eight years** of teaching and research experience
- Teaching experience associated with the **University of Ghana** and **University of Hamburg**
- Experience teaching students at beginner, intermediate and advanced levels across different countries
- Academic expertise in language teaching, linguistics, translation and curriculum development

Her wider international teaching experience is also documented in [The Africa Institute's Kiswahili faculty profile](https://www.theafricainstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/TAI_LC_Brochure__EN_-Spring-2026.pdf).

These experiences matter because a good language lesson needs more than a list of translations. Learners need to hear how people speak, understand why expressions are used and practise using them in real conversations.

## Bringing Kiswahili learning closer to Africans

A learner in Accra may want to visit Tanzania. A student in Lagos may be interested in East African music, business or culture. Someone in Nairobi may want to improve their formal Kiswahili. Their reasons differ, but the need is similar: **clear, useful language learning**.

Falowen began with German learning through Learn Language Education Academy in Ghana. Our growing Swahili programme brings the same focus on structured lessons and regular practice to another African language.

Dr. Ruth's experience connects teaching in **East Africa, West Africa and Europe**. It brings an important perspective to a platform that welcomes learners from different places and backgrounds.

## What is special about Dr. Ruth's teaching approach?

Dr. Ruth's work places emphasis on **student participation, cultural understanding and practical communication**.

That means learners should not simply memorise a sentence such as *Habari yako?* They should understand when to use it, listen to the response and try a short exchange themselves.

Her expertise includes:

- Explaining grammar in ways learners can use
- Developing speaking and listening confidence
- Connecting language with everyday situations and culture
- Supporting students through interactive, learner-focused instruction

Whether the goal is travel, study, professional communication or personal interest, these are useful foundations for learning Swahili.

## Learn Swahili online with Falowen

Falowen's **Swahili A1 beginner content** is being introduced progressively. The growing course includes practical activities such as vocabulary, grammar explanations, listening practice and workbook exercises.

For example, learners can start with everyday expressions:

| Kiswahili | English |
|---|---|
| Jambo! | Hello! |
| Habari yako? | How are you? |
| Nzuri, asante. | Fine, thank you. |
| Jina langu ni … | My name is … |
| Naomba maji. | I would like some water. |
| Karibu! | Welcome! |

Try saying the words aloud. Then use two or three in a short conversation.

You can also explore lessons built around practical situations, including buying items at a market, greeting people and asking simple questions. The course is growing, so available lessons and teaching arrangements can change as new material is added.

## Why learn Kiswahili if you live in Ghana, Nigeria or elsewhere in Africa?

You do not need to live in Tanzania or Kenya to have a reason to study Kiswahili.

Learning Swahili can help you:

- Communicate more confidently when travelling in parts of East Africa
- Explore African literature, music and everyday culture in another language
- Build connections with people from different African countries
- Develop a useful new language skill for study or personal growth

For West African learners, studying Kiswahili can also be a way to engage more closely with the cultural and linguistic diversity of the continent.
