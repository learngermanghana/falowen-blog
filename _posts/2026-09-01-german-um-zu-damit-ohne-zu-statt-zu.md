## How German Infinitive Clauses Make Your Sentences Better

Once you move beyond short beginner sentences, you need ways to connect ideas naturally. German structures such as **um ... zu**, **damit**, **ohne ... zu** and **statt ... zu** are especially useful because they help you explain purpose, alternatives and actions that do not happen.

Compare these two versions:

**Ich lerne jeden Tag. Ich möchte die Prüfung bestehen.**  
I study every day. I want to pass the exam.

A more natural sentence is:

**Ich lerne jeden Tag, um die Prüfung zu bestehen.**  
I study every day in order to pass the exam.

The meaning is similar, but the second sentence sounds more connected and mature.

## 1. Use um ... zu to Express a Purpose

**um ... zu** answers the question **Wozu?** — for what purpose?

The basic structure is:

**main clause + , um + other information + zu + infinitive**

Examples:

**Ich fahre nach Berlin, um meine Schwester zu besuchen.**  
I am travelling to Berlin to visit my sister.

**Wir sparen Geld, um ein Auto zu kaufen.**  
We are saving money in order to buy a car.

**Sie lernt Deutsch, um in Deutschland zu studieren.**  
She is learning German in order to study in Germany.

Notice that the infinitive comes at the end.

## 2. When Can You Use um ... zu?

A very important rule is that the understood subject of both actions should normally be the same.

**Ich gehe früh ins Bett, um morgen fit zu sein.**

The person who goes to bed is also the person who wants to be fit. Therefore, **um ... zu** works well.

But consider:

**Ich spreche langsam, damit die Schüler mich verstehen.**  
I speak slowly so that the students understand me.

Here, **ich** performs the first action and **die Schüler** perform the second. Because the subjects are different, we normally use **damit**, not *um ... zu*.

## 3. damit: Purpose with a Full Subordinate Clause

**damit** also expresses purpose, but it introduces a subordinate clause with its own subject and conjugated verb.

Structure:

**main clause + , damit + subject + ... + verb**

Examples:

**Ich schreibe die Adresse auf, damit du sie nicht vergisst.**  
I write down the address so that you do not forget it.

**Der Lehrer erklärt die Regel noch einmal, damit alle sie verstehen.**  
The teacher explains the rule again so that everyone understands it.

**Wir schließen das Fenster, damit es nicht zu kalt wird.**  
We close the window so that it does not get too cold.

Because **damit** introduces a subordinate clause, the conjugated verb moves to the end.

## 4. um ... zu or damit?

A useful shortcut is:

- Same subject → **um ... zu** is usually the natural choice.
- Different subjects → use **damit**.

Compare:

**Maria macht einen Deutschkurs, um ihre Sprachkenntnisse zu verbessern.**  
Maria takes a German course to improve her language skills.

**Maria spricht zu Hause Deutsch, damit ihre Kinder die Sprache lernen.**  
Maria speaks German at home so that her children learn the language.

This distinction is extremely useful in German writing exams because it helps you connect arguments without repeating short sentences.

## 5. ohne ... zu: Doing Something Without Another Action

Use **ohne ... zu** when the same subject does one action without doing another.

**Er ging weg, ohne etwas zu sagen.**  
He left without saying anything.

**Sie bestand die Prüfung, ohne viel zu lernen.**  
She passed the exam without studying much.

**Du kannst die Aufgabe lösen, ohne ein Wörterbuch zu benutzen.**  
You can solve the task without using a dictionary.

The infinitive again comes at the end.

## 6. statt ... zu: Instead of Doing Something

**statt ... zu** means **instead of doing something**.

**Er schaut fern, statt seine Hausaufgaben zu machen.**  
He watches television instead of doing his homework.

**Sie fährt mit dem Auto, statt den Bus zu nehmen.**  
She drives instead of taking the bus.

**Wir sollten das Problem lösen, statt darüber zu streiten.**  
We should solve the problem instead of arguing about it.

You may also see **anstatt ... zu**. It has essentially the same meaning.

## 7. What Happens with Separable Verbs?

With a separable verb, **zu** goes between the prefix and the verb stem.

**einkaufen → einzukaufen**

**Ich gehe in die Stadt, um einzukaufen.**  
I am going into town to shop.

**anrufen → anzurufen**

**Sie schreibt sich die Nummer auf, um später anzurufen.**  
She writes down the number in order to call later.

But inseparable verbs such as **besuchen** do not insert *zu* inside the verb:

**Ich fahre nach Hamburg, um meine Freunde zu besuchen.**

## 8. Modal Verbs and Infinitive Clauses

Learners sometimes try to carry every modal verb into the infinitive clause. Often, the purpose can be expressed more simply.

Instead of:

**Ich lerne viel. Ich möchte die Prüfung bestehen.**

write:

**Ich lerne viel, um die Prüfung zu bestehen.**

This is one reason **um ... zu** is so valuable from A2 onward: it reduces repetition and makes your writing more efficient.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Mistake 1: Using um ... zu with different subjects

Incorrect:

**Ich erkläre es langsam, um du alles zu verstehen.**

Correct:

**Ich erkläre es langsam, damit du alles verstehst.**

### Mistake 2: Forgetting zu

Incorrect:

**Wir fahren nach München, um unsere Freunde besuchen.**

Correct:

**Wir fahren nach München, um unsere Freunde zu besuchen.**

### Mistake 3: Wrong word order after damit

Incorrect:

**Ich wiederhole die Regel, damit alle verstehen sie.**

Correct:

**Ich wiederhole die Regel, damit alle sie verstehen.**

Remember: **damit** creates a subordinate clause, so the conjugated verb goes to the end.

## A Practical Learning Method

Do not memorize these structures as four isolated grammar rules. Learn the meaning each one contributes:

**Purpose, same subject:** um ... zu  
**Purpose, separate subject possible:** damit  
**Without doing something:** ohne ... zu  
**Instead of doing something:** statt ... zu

Then practise transforming pairs of short sentences into one connected sentence.

For example:

**Ich mache Notizen. Ich möchte die neuen Wörter nicht vergessen.**

→ **Ich mache Notizen, um die neuen Wörter nicht zu vergessen.**

This type of transformation trains grammar and writing at the same time.

## Mini Practice

Complete each sentence with **um ... zu**, **damit**, **ohne ... zu** or **statt ... zu**.

1. Ich lerne neue Wörter, ______ meinen Wortschatz zu erweitern.
2. Der Lehrer spricht deutlich, ______ die Anfänger ihn verstehen.
3. Paul verließ das Zimmer, ______ sich zu verabschieden.
4. Sie beschwert sich ständig, ______ nach einer Lösung zu suchen.

### Answers

1. **um**
2. **damit**
3. **ohne**
4. **statt**

## Why These Structures Matter from A2 to B2

At A1, you can communicate a lot with short main clauses. As you progress, however, German requires you to show relationships between ideas. Purpose, contrast, alternatives and consequences become increasingly important in conversations, emails and exam writing.

Using **um ... zu**, **damit**, **ohne ... zu** and **statt ... zu** correctly gives you several reliable ways to build longer sentences without making them unnecessarily complicated.

The goal is not simply to produce long sentences. The goal is to express the relationship between your ideas clearly.
