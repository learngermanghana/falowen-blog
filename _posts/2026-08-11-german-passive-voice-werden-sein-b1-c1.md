The German **passive voice (Passiv)** becomes increasingly important from B1 onward. You will meet it in news reports, formal writing, workplace German, academic texts and German exams. The good news is that the basic structure is much easier once you understand one central idea: in a passive sentence, the **action or result is more important than the person doing it**.

Compare:

- **Die Firma produziert die Geräte.** — The company produces the devices.
- **Die Geräte werden produziert.** — The devices are produced.

The first sentence focuses on *die Firma*. The second focuses on *die Geräte* and the action.

## 1. Vorgangspassiv: werden + Partizip II

The most common German passive describes a **process or action**. It is called **Vorgangspassiv**.

The basic formula is:

**werden + Partizip II**

Examples:

- **Das Essen wird vorbereitet.** — The food is being prepared.
- **Die Straße wird repariert.** — The road is being repaired.
- **Neue Wohnungen werden gebaut.** — New apartments are being built.

Notice that **werden** changes according to the subject, while the past participle normally stands at the end.

## 2. Turning an active sentence into passive

Start with this active sentence:

**Die Lehrerin erklärt die Grammatik.** — The teacher explains the grammar.

The accusative object **die Grammatik** becomes the subject of the passive sentence:

**Die Grammatik wird erklärt.** — The grammar is explained.

If the person performing the action matters, you can add **von + Dativ**:

**Die Grammatik wird von der Lehrerin erklärt.** — The grammar is explained by the teacher.

A useful learner rule is therefore:

**Active:** someone does something.  
**Passive:** something is done.

## 3. Passive voice in different tenses

You should not learn the passive only in the present tense. B1–C1 texts frequently use several forms.

### Präsens

**Der Antrag wird geprüft.** — The application is being checked.

### Präteritum

**Der Antrag wurde geprüft.** — The application was checked.

### Perfekt

**Der Antrag ist geprüft worden.** — The application has been checked.

Be careful here: the passive perfect uses **worden**, not *geworden*.

### Futur I

**Der Antrag wird morgen geprüft werden.** — The application will be checked tomorrow.

This can look intimidating because **wird** and **werden** appear in the same sentence. Break it into parts: *wird* marks the future, while *geprüft werden* is the passive infinitive.

## 4. Vorgangspassiv vs. Zustandspassiv

Advanced learners must distinguish an **action** from a **state resulting from an action**.

### Vorgangspassiv: werden + Partizip II

**Die Tür wird geschlossen.** — The door is being closed.

Someone is closing it. The action is happening.

### Zustandspassiv: sein + Partizip II

**Die Tür ist geschlossen.** — The door is closed.

Here, the action is no longer the focus. We are describing the resulting state.

Another pair:

- **Das Fenster wird geöffnet.** — The window is being opened.
- **Das Fenster ist geöffnet.** — The window is open.

Ask yourself: **Is something happening, or am I describing the condition afterward?** That question usually tells you whether to choose *werden* or *sein*.

## 5. Passive sentences with modal verbs

Modal verbs are especially useful at B2 and C1 because they let you discuss rules, possibilities and obligations.

Structure:

**Modalverb + ... + Partizip II + werden**

Examples:

- **Das Problem muss gelöst werden.** — The problem must be solved.
- **Die Kosten können gesenkt werden.** — The costs can be reduced.
- **Neue Maßnahmen sollten eingeführt werden.** — New measures should be introduced.

These structures are excellent for formal arguments because they allow you to focus on what should happen without repeatedly naming a person or institution.

## 6. Useful passive structures for B2 and C1 exams

Instead of always writing:

**Die Regierung muss strengere Regeln einführen.**

You can write:

**Strengere Regeln müssen eingeführt werden.**

Instead of:

**Unternehmen sollten ihre Mitarbeiter besser unterstützen.**

Try:

**Mitarbeiter sollten besser unterstützt werden.**

For a more formal C1 style:

**Es sollte stärker darauf geachtet werden, dass alle Beschäftigten Zugang zu Weiterbildung haben.**  
— Greater attention should be paid to ensuring that all employees have access to further training.

Passive constructions can make argumentative writing more impersonal and formal, but do not use them in every sentence. Strong German writing mixes active and passive structures naturally.

## 7. Passive without a normal subject

German can also form impersonal passive sentences:

- **Hier wird Deutsch gesprochen.** — German is spoken here.
- **Im Unterricht wird viel diskutiert.** — There is a lot of discussion in class.
- **Es wird nach einer Lösung gesucht.** — A solution is being sought.

The sentence **Hier wird Deutsch gesprochen** is especially useful because it shows that German passive does not always need the kind of subject English learners expect.

## Common mistakes to avoid

### Mistake 1: forgetting the participle

Incorrect: **Das Haus wird bauen.**  
Correct: **Das Haus wird gebaut.**

### Mistake 2: confusing sein and werden

**Das Geschäft wird geschlossen** describes the process of closing.  
**Das Geschäft ist geschlossen** describes its state: it is closed.

### Mistake 3: using geworden in passive perfect

Incorrect: **Das Haus ist gebaut geworden.**  
Correct: **Das Haus ist gebaut worden.**

### Mistake 4: translating English word for word

Do not simply look for an English form of “to be + past participle.” First decide whether German needs **Vorgangspassiv**, **Zustandspassiv**, or a natural active sentence.

## A practical way to learn the passive

When you learn a new verb, create three related sentences. For example with **reparieren**:

1. **Der Mechaniker repariert das Auto.** — The mechanic repairs the car.
2. **Das Auto wird repariert.** — The car is being repaired.
3. **Das Auto ist repariert.** — The car is repaired / in a repaired state.

This trains active voice, process passive and state passive together instead of treating them as isolated grammar rules.

## Mini practice

Change these active sentences into passive sentences before checking the answers.

1. Die Firma entwickelt eine neue App.  
2. Die Regierung muss das Problem lösen.  
3. Die Schule organisiert den Kurs.

Possible answers:

1. **Eine neue App wird entwickelt.**
2. **Das Problem muss gelöst werden.**
3. **Der Kurs wird organisiert.**

Now create one passive sentence about your studies, one about work and one about society. This makes the grammar relevant to vocabulary you already use.

## From B1 to C1: what should you master?

At **B1**, concentrate on present and past passive forms and understand why the object of an active sentence can become the subject of a passive sentence.

At **B2**, become comfortable with passive constructions using modal verbs and use them in formal writing and discussions.

At **C1**, distinguish confidently between Vorgangspassiv and Zustandspassiv, recognize passive alternatives in complex texts, and choose active or passive voice according to emphasis and style.
