## A1 Berlin Klasse — German for Beginners

The **A1 Berlin Klasse** is designed for beginners building their first German foundation. At A1, learners work on essential communication such as introducing themselves, asking and answering simple questions, talking about family and daily life, understanding basic instructions, and forming correct beginner sentences.

Typical A1 German includes expressions such as:

**Ich heiße Ama und ich komme aus Ghana.**  
*My name is Ama and I come from Ghana.*

**Ich lerne Deutsch, weil ich in Deutschland studieren möchte.**  
*I am learning German because I would like to study in Germany.*

A strong A1 foundation is important because later grammar and communication skills build on what you learn at beginner level.

## A1 Hamburg Klasse — Another A1 Option

The **A1 Hamburg Klasse** gives prospective beginners another upcoming A1 option. Having more than one A1 class can make it easier for students to find an available intake that suits their plans.

A1 is suitable if you are new to German or need to rebuild your beginner foundation before progressing to A2. Learners should aim to develop all four core skills: **Lesen (reading), Hören (listening), Schreiben (writing) and Sprechen (speaking)**.

Do not focus only on memorising vocabulary. Learn words inside useful sentences. Instead of learning only *der Termin* (appointment), practise a complete expression such as:

**Ich habe morgen einen Termin.**  
*I have an appointment tomorrow.*

This makes vocabulary easier to use in real conversations and examinations.

## Upcoming A2 German Class in Ghana

The upcoming **A2 German class** is intended for learners who already have an A1 foundation. At A2, German becomes more practical and connected. You learn to describe experiences, communicate in common everyday situations and build longer sentences.

For example:

**Letztes Wochenende habe ich meine Freunde besucht.**  
*Last weekend I visited my friends.*

**Ich lerne jeden Tag Deutsch, damit ich sicherer sprechen kann.**  
*I study German every day so that I can speak more confidently.*

Before joining A2, make sure you are reasonably comfortable with A1 basics such as present-tense conjugation, modal verbs, articles, accusative and dative foundations, separable verbs and basic past-tense communication.

## Tips Before You Register for a German Course

First, choose the correct level. If you have never studied German, A1 is normally the appropriate starting point. If you have completed A1, review your foundation before moving into A2.

Second, check whether the current class schedule fits your weekly routine. German requires regular attendance and independent practice, so choose a class you can realistically follow.

Third, practise from the beginning. Even 20–30 minutes of focused German practice on non-class days can help you retain vocabulary, grammar and sentence patterns.

Finally, do not wait until an examination is close before practising speaking and writing. Build these skills throughout your course.
